Random-walk Security Simulation

Simulate m server trajectories over n weeks. Each week a server is breached with probability p (breach => -1) and secure with probability 1-p (secure => +1). We plot cumulative trajectories, count final total scores, and compare the empirical distribution to the theoretical binomial distribution.

Quick math / mapping to binomial

If a trajectory has k secure weeks (so n-k breaches) then the total score after n weeks is

S = (+1)*k + (-1)*(n-k) = 2k - n

Therefore, counting trajectories by total score S is equivalent to counting the binomial number of successes k with success probability q = 1 - p. The theoretical probability mass function for S is obtained from Binomial(n, q) by mapping k => S = 2k - n.

Controls

Tip: increase m to see empirical counts converge to the theoretical binomial curve. Increase n to see the distribution concentrate (CLT / law of large numbers).

Explanation of what you will see
  1. Trajectories plot — each line is the cumulative score S_t after t weeks for one simulated server. When secure add +1, when breached add -1.
  2. Histogram — counts of final scores S_n across the m runs. We overlay the theoretical expected counts from a Binomial(n, 1-p) distribution (converted to score space).
  3. Convergence — as m → large, the empirical histogram approaches the theoretical bars. As n grows, the possible score range grows and (after normalization) the distribution approaches Gaussian shape via the CLT.
